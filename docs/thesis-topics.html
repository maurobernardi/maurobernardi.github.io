Thesis opportunities

A good thesis starts with a question whose scope fits the student’s preparation and the time available. The themes below are indicative directions for discussion, rather than a fixed project specification. We can refine the question, methodological depth and balance between theory, computation and application together.

Bachelor’s projects can focus on understanding and comparing established methods. Master’s projects can develop a more substantial methodological or computational investigation. Doctoral topics provide broader research directions, developed in coordination with the relevant programme and admission process.

Bachelor’s thesis topics

Comparing regularized regression methods

Study how coefficient shrinkage and variable selection change fitted models and prediction on simple simulated datasets.

Research question

How do the amount of shrinkage and the correlation between predictors affect variable selection and prediction?

Suggested preparation

Introductory regression, basic probability and familiarity with R.

Possible approach

A focused comparison of a small set of methods, with reproducible code and interpretation of the results.

Possible outcome

A reproducible R analysis with a small simulation study, coefficient-path plots and a clear discussion of prediction and selection.

Mean and quantile regression for housing prices

Compare a conditional-mean analysis with regression at a small number of quantiles, explaining how the statistical targets differ.

Research question

Which features of a housing-price distribution are hidden by an analysis of the conditional mean alone?

Suggested preparation

Introductory statistics, linear regression and basic R programming.

Possible approach

A guided data analysis, supported by a modest simulation illustrating interpretation and prediction.

Possible outcome

A documented analysis comparing mean regression with a few quantile levels, including graphical interpretation and a concise account of uncertainty.

Master’s thesis topics

Adaptive penalties and degrees of freedom

Investigate how response-dependent weights affect model complexity and model-selection criteria in penalized regression.

Research question

How does the response dependence of adaptive weights alter the local sensitivity of a fitted model and the criteria used to select it?

Suggested preparation

Regression, linear algebra, optimization and R programming.

Possible approach

A methodological study combining derivations, implementation and controlled numerical experiments.

Possible outcome

A carefully stated derivation for a specified model, numerical checks and a reproducible comparison of selection criteria.

Interpretable functional regression

Explore local sparsity in functional regression and its consequences for interpreting estimated coefficient functions.

Research question

When does local sparsity make a coefficient function easier to interpret without sacrificing predictive accuracy?

Suggested preparation

Regression, matrix algebra and an introduction to functional data analysis.

Possible approach

Basis representations, sparsity-promoting estimation and an application with functional responses or covariates.

Possible outcome

An implemented basis-expansion model, coefficient-function visualizations and a sensitivity analysis of the regularization choice.

Variational inference for hierarchical models

Study a variational approximation for a well-defined hierarchical model and assess its numerical behavior and uncertainty estimates.

Research question

Which posterior features are accurately retained by a tractable variational approximation, and where does the approximation lose uncertainty?

Suggested preparation

Bayesian statistics, probability, matrix calculus and scientific programming.

Possible approach

Derivation of updates, implementation and comparisons against an appropriate reference on manageable examples.

Possible outcome

An implementation with convergence diagnostics and a comparison against a suitable sampling or numerical reference on manageable examples.

PhD research directions

Scalable Bayesian computation for structured models

Develop computational methods for Bayesian models with latent, hierarchical or dynamic structure, examining approximation accuracy alongside scalability.

Research question

How can computation exploit latent structure while retaining useful posterior information as model dimension grows?

Suggested preparation

Strong preparation in probability, statistical inference, linear algebra and computation.

Possible approach

A research programme combining algorithmic development, theoretical analysis and carefully designed validation.

Possible outcome

A sequence of methodological contributions, supported by explicit approximation diagnostics, computational comparisons and applications.

Model selection and uncertainty under adaptive regularization

Investigate the interaction between adaptive penalties, selection, sensitivity of fitted maps and measures of model complexity.

Research question

Which notions of complexity and uncertainty remain informative after adaptive regularization and data-dependent selection?

Suggested preparation

Advanced statistical inference, optimization and mathematical statistics.

Possible approach

Development of new results and computational methods, with attention to the assumptions needed for uncertainty assessment.

Possible outcome

Theoretical and computational contributions addressing a clearly delimited selection problem and the assumptions required for valid conclusions.

Structured quantile and functional regression

Develop flexible regression methods for distributional features of structured data, with interpretable functional, spatial or hierarchical effects.

Research question

How can quantile and functional regression represent structured heterogeneity while remaining interpretable and computationally feasible?

Suggested preparation

Advanced regression, asymptotic or Bayesian methods, and scientific computing.

Possible approach

Methodological research integrating estimation, computational algorithms and theory-driven empirical assessment.

Possible outcome

New estimation or inferential methods, accompanied by reproducible studies of structured data and a clear account of their scope.

Discuss a project

Please email me with your degree programme, relevant courses, programming experience, interests and expected thesis period. A short description of what you would like to understand is a useful starting point; a fully developed proposal is not required for an initial discussion.

What a project involves

An initial discussion establishes a question, a feasible scope and the background needed to make progress. The balance between mathematical development, programming and data analysis depends on the project. A reproducible analysis and a careful account of assumptions and limitations are common expectations across these different forms of work.

The supervision record illustrates previous topics, while the software guides provide examples of the style of analysis a project may develop. Doctoral directions are research themes for discussion and do not constitute advertised funded positions; admission and funding follow the relevant programme’s procedures.
