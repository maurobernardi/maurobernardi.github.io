Selected readings in statistics

Statistical ideas are often best understood by reading a foundational account alongside the paper in which a particular method was introduced. This selection brings together books and articles on inference, statistical learning and computation, with particular attention to the themes that recur in my research and teaching. The notes suggest what to look for in each reference and how the readings relate to one another.

This is a thematic reading collection rather than a ranking of recent publications. For newly collected papers, see the separate arXiv reading list. Links lead to publishers or author-maintained pages, including freely available versions where the authors provide them.

Statistical foundations and learning

Statistical Inference

George Casella and Roger L. Berger. Book, second edition, Chapman & Hall/CRC reprint (2024). Publisher and bibliographic record.

A systematic foundation for estimation, hypothesis testing and confidence intervals. The development from probability models to sufficient statistics and decision criteria helps explain why an inferential procedure works and which assumptions support its interpretation.

All of Statistics: A Concise Course in Statistical Inference

Larry Wasserman (2004). Book, Springer. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A compact bridge between probability, classical inference and modern statistical methods. It offers a useful first overview before turning to more specialised treatments of asymptotic theory, nonparametric estimation or statistical learning.

Asymptotic Statistics

A. W. van der Vaart (1998). Book, Cambridge University Press. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A reference for understanding how estimators and tests behave as the amount of information grows. The treatment of likelihood methods, M-estimation and empirical processes provides the theoretical language needed to assess the conditions behind an asymptotic claim, including its limits in a particular modelling problem.

An Introduction to Statistical Learning: with Applications in R

Gareth James, Daniela Witten, Trevor Hastie and Robert Tibshirani (2021). Book, second edition, Springer. Authors’ page, book and supporting material.

An accessible introduction to regression, classification, resampling and regularisation, with examples in R. Its emphasis on interpretation and model assessment makes it a natural starting point before the more mathematical treatment in The Elements of Statistical Learning.

The Elements of Statistical Learning

Trevor Hastie, Robert Tibshirani and Jerome Friedman (2009). Book, second edition, Springer. Authors’ page and book.

A broad account of prediction, regularisation and statistical learning. Read the discussion of model assessment alongside the chapters on linear methods: it helps connect the construction of a fitted model with the separate question of how its predictive performance should be evaluated.

All of Nonparametric Statistics

Larry Wasserman (2006). Book, Springer. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A treatment of estimation when a finite-dimensional parametric family is too restrictive. Density estimation, nonparametric regression and smoothing provide a setting in which to understand the balance between approximation error, variability and the amount of regularisation.

Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning

Christopher M. Bishop (2006). Book, Springer. Author’s page and book.

A probabilistic treatment of learning that connects regression and classification with latent-variable models, graphical models and approximate inference. The common probabilistic framework helps explain relationships among methods that are often presented as separate algorithms.

Probabilistic Machine Learning: An Introduction

Kevin P. Murphy (2022). Book, MIT Press. Author’s page, book and code.

A broad introduction to machine learning through probability, statistical decision theory and computation. It complements Bishop with a more recent treatment of learning methods and provides a route from familiar statistical models to neural networks and modern predictive modelling.

Statistical models

Statistical modelling connects a scientific question with assumptions about how observations arise and depend on one another. These books develop that connection from general principles to regression, hierarchical structures and temporal dependence, while keeping estimation, uncertainty and model assessment in view.

Statistical Models

A. C. Davison (2003). Book, Cambridge University Press. Publisher and bibliographic record.

An integrated account of statistical modelling and inference that brings probability models, likelihood and regression into a common framework. It provides a foundation for understanding specialised model classes as extensions of general statistical principles.

Generalized Linear Models

Peter McCullagh and John A. Nelder (1989). Book, second edition, Chapman & Hall. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A foundational account of regression models built from a response distribution, a linear predictor and a link function. The treatment of likelihood, deviance and diagnostics explains how inference and model assessment extend beyond Gaussian linear regression.

Data Analysis Using Regression and Multilevel/Hierarchical Models

Andrew Gelman and Jennifer Hill (2007). Book, Cambridge University Press. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A modelling-oriented introduction to regression and multilevel analysis. The progression from ordinary regression to varying intercepts and slopes makes partial pooling concrete and connects hierarchical assumptions with the interpretation of fitted effects.

Mixed-Effects Models in S and S-PLUS

José C. Pinheiro and Douglas M. Bates (2000). Book, Springer. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A detailed treatment of linear and nonlinear mixed-effects models for grouped and longitudinal observations. It is especially useful for connecting random-effects specifications, within-group covariance structures and the computational methods used to fit them.

Generalized Additive Models: An Introduction with R

Simon N. Wood (2017). Book, second edition, Chapman & Hall/CRC. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A development of regression models in which smooth functions describe covariate effects. Basis representations, penalties and smoothing-parameter estimation are treated together, clarifying how flexibility is controlled and how uncertainty is assessed.

Time Series Analysis by State Space Methods

James Durbin and Siem Jan Koopman (2012). Book, second edition, Oxford University Press. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A systematic account of dynamic models expressed through unobserved states and observation equations. Filtering and smoothing connect the model specification with estimation and forecasting, while extensions show how the framework accommodates more complex observation processes.

Gaussian Markov Random Fields: Theory and Applications

Håvard Rue and Leonhard Held (2005). Book, Chapman & Hall/CRC. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A foundation for Gaussian models whose conditional independence structure is encoded by a sparse precision matrix. The interplay between probabilistic structure and sparse matrix computations is central to understanding both spatial models and efficient inference for latent Gaussian models.

These readings complement the modelling topics in Teaching, including the course on statistical models and the materials on hierarchical data.

Generalized linear mixed models

Generalized linear mixed models combine non-Gaussian response distributions with random effects that describe heterogeneity and dependence within groups. These readings complement the books on linear mixed models above, with greater emphasis on likelihood integration, estimation and the interpretation of conditional effects.

Generalized, Linear, and Mixed Models

Charles E. McCulloch, Shayle R. Searle and John M. Neuhaus (2008). Book, second edition, Wiley. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A unified treatment of linear models, generalised linear models and mixed models for correlated observations. The development of estimation and inference makes it particularly useful for understanding how random effects change both the likelihood and the interpretation of regression parameters.

Generalized Linear Mixed Models: Modern Concepts, Methods and Applications

Walter W. Stroup, Marina Ptukhina and Julie Garai (2024). Book, second edition, Chapman & Hall/CRC. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A modelling-oriented account that uses the GLMM framework to connect response distributions, study design and random-effects structures. The applications help relate inferential choices to the questions posed by an experiment or observational study; computational illustrations use SAS.

Linear and Generalized Linear Mixed Models and Their Applications

Jiming Jiang (2007). Book, Springer. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A more theoretical treatment of estimation, prediction and inference in mixed models. It is a useful companion when the aim is to understand the mathematical justification of procedures and the additional difficulties introduced by non-Gaussian responses.

Extending the Linear Model with R: Generalized Linear, Mixed Effects and Nonparametric Regression Models

Julian J. Faraway (2016). Book, second edition, Chapman & Hall/CRC. Publisher and bibliographic record.

An accessible bridge from linear regression to generalised linear and mixed-effects models, supported by worked analyses in R. It offers a practical entry point before the more specialised GLMM texts, while also introducing nonparametric extensions of regression.

Sparsity and adaptive regularisation

Regression Shrinkage and Selection Via the Lasso

Robert Tibshirani (1996). Article, Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B, 58(1), 267–288. Article.

The original lasso paper connects coefficient shrinkage with variable selection through an absolute-value penalty. It is a useful starting point for considering how a penalty changes both the geometry of estimation and the interpretation of a regression fit.

Regularization and Variable Selection Via the Elastic Net

Hui Zou and Trevor Hastie (2005). Article, Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B, 67(2), 301–320. Article.

This paper develops a combined penalty for settings in which predictors may be strongly correlated. Reading it after the lasso paper clarifies why different regularisation choices can produce different selection behaviour even when they address the same prediction problem.

The Adaptive Lasso and Its Oracle Properties

Hui Zou (2006). Article, Journal of the American Statistical Association, 101(476), 1418–1429. Article.

Adaptive weighting links a preliminary estimate to the penalty used in a subsequent fit. The asymptotic results are particularly instructive when read with close attention to the tuning and regularity assumptions: oracle properties are conditional theoretical statements, rather than a guarantee for every finite dataset.

These papers provide methodological background for the paths and model-selection examples in SPARES.

Bayesian inference and approximation

Bayesian Data Analysis

Andrew Gelman, John B. Carlin, Hal S. Stern, David B. Dunson, Aki Vehtari and Donald B. Rubin (2013). Book, third edition, CRC Press. Authors’ page, book and supporting material.

A foundation for building Bayesian models and assessing what their posterior distributions say about data and uncertainty. The connections among hierarchical modelling, posterior predictive checking and computation are especially useful when moving beyond a simple regression specification.

Monte Carlo Statistical Methods

Christian P. Robert and George Casella (2004). Book, second edition, Springer. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A foundation for simulation-based statistical computation, from Monte Carlo integration and importance sampling to Markov chain methods. It develops the principles needed to understand how simulation approximates posterior quantities and how the quality of those approximations can be assessed.

Variational Inference: A Review for Statisticians

David M. Blei, Alp Kucukelbir and Jon D. McAuliffe (2017). Article, Journal of the American Statistical Association, 112(518), 859–877. Article · Author-hosted version.

An introduction to posterior approximation through optimisation, including the evidence lower bound and mean-field approximations. Read it with two questions in mind: which dependencies does an approximation retain, and how might the approximation affect uncertainty quantification?

Examples of Bayesian modelling and posterior summaries appear in the fastBVS and bquantreg guides; the research page includes related work on variational methods.

Quantile regression

Quantile Regression

Roger Koenker (2005). Book, Cambridge University Press. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A systematic treatment of regression for conditional quantiles, encompassing interpretation, estimation and inference. It provides the background needed to understand why an effect can vary across a conditional distribution, and why a quantile regression coefficient answers a different question from a conditional-mean coefficient.

Quantile Regression: Theory and Applications

Cristina Davino, Marilena Furno and Domenico Vistocco (2014). Book, Wiley. Publisher and bibliographic record.

An applied account of quantile regression that connects its geometrical interpretation with estimation, inference and model diagnostics. The worked analyses help translate a collection of quantile-specific coefficients into substantive statements about a conditional distribution.

Quantile Regression: Estimation and Simulation

Marilena Furno and Domenico Vistocco (2018). Book, volume 2, Wiley. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A continuation into more specialised questions, including robustness, related distributional regression methods and time series. Its example-based development is useful for exploring how modelling choices affect estimates and for examining statistical behaviour through simulation.

The bquantreg guide connects conditional-quantile modelling with worked analyses of housing prices and comparisons among estimation methods.

Functional data analysis

Introduction to Functional Data Analysis

Piotr Kokoszka and Matthew Reimherr (2017). Book, Chapman & Hall/CRC. Authors’ page and supporting material.

An introduction to statistical methods for observations that take the form of curves. The progression from functional representations and dimension reduction to regression provides an accessible route into the subject, supported by computational examples.

Functional Data Analysis

James O. Ramsay and Bernard W. Silverman (2005). Book, second edition, Springer. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A foundation for treating curves as statistical observations, with smoothing, basis representations and functional regression as central tools. The representation of a function and the regularisation imposed on it should be read together: both influence what can be learned from discretely observed data.

Nonparametric Functional Data Analysis: Theory and Practice

Frédéric Ferraty and Philippe Vieu (2006). Book, Springer. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A treatment of nonparametric methods for functional observations, with particular attention to regression. The choice of a distance or semimetric between curves makes explicit which features of an observed function are relevant to prediction.

Inference for Functional Data with Applications

Lajos Horváth and Piotr Kokoszka (2012). Book, Springer. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A more advanced treatment of inference for functional observations, including functional linear models, hypothesis testing and dependent curves. It complements introductory texts by developing the probabilistic arguments behind inferential procedures and their applications.

The fdaSP guide connects functional regression with basis representations, local sparsity and reproducible analyses.

Optimisation and numerical methods

Numerical Analysis for Statisticians

Kenneth Lange (2010). Book, second edition, Springer. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A statistical perspective on numerical analysis, connecting linear algebra, approximation and optimisation with the calculations required by statistical methods. It provides a useful foundation for understanding why an algorithm works, how its numerical behaviour is assessed and which computational choices affect a fitted model.

Matrix Computations

Gene H. Golub and Charles F. Van Loan (2013). Book, fourth edition, Johns Hopkins University Press. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A comprehensive reference on numerical linear algebra, including matrix factorisations, least-squares problems and eigenvalue computations. Its treatment of conditioning and stability helps distinguish an algebraically valid formula from a reliable numerical implementation.

Convex Optimization

Stephen Boyd and Lieven Vandenberghe (2004). Book, Cambridge University Press. Authors’ page, book and exercises.

A reference for convexity, duality and numerical optimisation. For statistical work, the discussion of optimality conditions is particularly useful: it connects a penalised objective with the equations that define a fitted model and with the conditions an algorithm must satisfy.

MM Optimization Algorithms

Kenneth Lange (2016). Book, SIAM. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A systematic treatment of majorisation–minimisation and minorisation–maximisation methods. The construction of tractable surrogate objectives shows how difficult optimisation problems can be approached through simpler iterative updates, with statistical applications providing the motivation.

Distributed Optimization and Statistical Learning via the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers

Stephen Boyd, Neal Parikh, Eric Chu, Borja Peleato and Jonathan Eckstein (2011). Article, Foundations and Trends in Machine Learning, 3(1), 1–122. Article.

A detailed account of ADMM, including splitting strategies and stopping criteria. It provides a bridge from an optimisation formulation to an implementation, and helps explain why convergence diagnostics and the scaling of an objective matter when comparing statistical routines.

Numerical Methods for Least Squares Problems

Åke Björck (1996). Book, SIAM. Publisher and bibliographic record.

A numerical perspective on least-squares estimation, with attention to conditioning and the stability of matrix computations. Read alongside the fastQR guide to connect factorisations and matrix updates with repeated statistical calculations.

A possible reading route

For a broad introduction to inference, begin with All of Statistics or Statistical Inference, then use Asymptotic Statistics to study the conditions behind large-sample arguments. For statistical learning, move from An Introduction to Statistical Learning to The Elements of Statistical Learning and the original papers on sparsity and adaptive weighting.

For modelling, Statistical Models provides a general foundation before the books on generalised linear, multilevel, additive and state space models. For Bayesian computation, work from Bayesian Data Analysis to the variational inference review. For quantile or functional regression, begin with the corresponding monograph before moving to a specialised application. The optimisation and numerical references support each of these routes when the focus turns to implementation.
