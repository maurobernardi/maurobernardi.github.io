Mauro Bernardi · University of Padova

Collaborations

Much of my research develops through joint work on statistical methodology, computation and applications. The collaborations below bring together shared methodological interests and joint work, with links to related publications where available. They illustrate shared research interests across projects and career stages, rather than an exhaustive list of collaborators or a statement that every project is currently active.

Doctoral and thesis collaborations are also documented on the Students & Supervision page. The research page provides the bibliographic details and author lists for the work mentioned here.

Variational inference for non-regular likelihoods

Joint work with Cristian Castiglione develops variational methods for models with non-conjugate and non-regular likelihoods, including formulations based on non-smooth loss functions. The approach connects approximate Bayesian inference with mixed-effects models, quantile regression and support vector machines, without requiring model-specific data augmentation.

Non-Conjugate Variational Bayes for Pseudo-Likelihood Mixed Effect Models · arXiv version

Quantile regression and spatial modelling

Joint work with Cristian Castiglione, Eleonora Arnone, Alessio Farcomeni and Laura M. Sangalli develops spatial quantile regression with differential regularisation. This framework represents spatial variation while accommodating the geometry of the domain and information about anisotropy and non-stationarity. With Marco F. De Sanctis, Ilenia Di Battista, Alessandro Palummo and Francesca Ieva, collaborations within this group also investigate multiple spatial quantiles and applications to environmental data.

  • PDE-regularised spatial quantile regression, Journal of Multivariate Analysis, 205 (2025), 105381.
  • Exploring nitrogen dioxide spatial concentration via physics-informed multiple quantile regression, Environmental and Ecological Statistics, 32 (2025), 855–892.

Bayesian dynamic models and variable selection

Joint work with Nicolas Bianco and Daniele Bianchi concerns Bayesian inference for high-dimensional time series, including large vector autoregressions and dynamic variable selection. Variational approximations provide computational methods for estimating these models.

Large Bayesian VARs · Dynamic variable selection

Functional regression and local sparsity

With Antonio Canale and Marco Stefanucci, research on functional regression examines how regularisation can produce interpretable coefficient functions and surfaces. The methodological work is accompanied by software and examples in fdaSP.

Locally sparse function-on-function regression · Interpretable functional linear regression · fdaSP

Quantile models and economic applications

Work with Roberto Casarin, Bertrand B. Maillet and Lea Petrella studies dynamic quantile model averaging. Other collaborations include Marco Bottone and Lea Petrella on Bayesian quantile models and risk measures, Ghislaine Gayraud and Lea Petrella on tail-risk interdependence, and Mariangela Guidolin on spatial quantile modelling of rental prices.

Dynamic model averaging · Bayesian quantile regression · Tail-risk interdependence · Airbnb prices

Sports data and longitudinal modelling

Collaborations with Mattia Stival, Petros Dellaportas and Manuela Cattelan examine statistical questions arising from athletes’ performance records, including dependence over time, missing-data patterns and changes in physical measurements.

Changepoint detection · Missing data in runners’ careers

Networks

Collaboration with Monia Lupparelli concerns statistical networks and the modelling of dependence among variables. The focus is on understanding network structure and its statistical representation.

High-dimensional covariance estimation

Joint work with Alberto Roverato, Manuela Cattelan and Claudio Busatto concerns sparse estimation of variance–covariance matrices in high-dimensional settings. This research connects regularisation with computational methods for estimating large structured matrices.

Numerical methods for statistical computing

Joint work with Claudio Busatto and Manuela Cattelan develops fast matrix-updating methods for statistical applications. This computational perspective connects numerical linear algebra with the repeated fitting and exploration of regression models.

QR updating methods · fastQR

Dependence, risk and forecasting

Research with Paola Stolfi and Davide Vergni considers robust estimation of time-varying dependence, while work with Leopoldo Catania examines the comparison of risk forecasts. A collaboration with Francesco Lisi develops point and interval forecasts for electricity prices and demand.

Time-dependent precision matrices · Risk-model comparison · Electricity prices and demand
